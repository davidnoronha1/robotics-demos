3. IMU attitude estimation

Attitude estimates from your own phone's sensors — gyro-only, accel-only, complementary — and a full 6-DOF pose EKF that estimates position and velocity alongside orientation. Pick up your phone and feel why fusion exists.

Frames & why quaternions

The phone has a body frame (X right, Y up the screen, Z out of the glass) and the room has a world frame (X north, Z up). We store the attitude as a unit quaternion $q$, which rotates body vectors into world vectors:

$$\mathbf{a}_{\text{world}} = q\, \mathbf{a}_{\text{body}}\, q^{-1}$$

We never use Euler angles internally — at pitch $\pm 90°$ two axes line up and one angle becomes undefined (gimbal lock). Quaternions have no such singularity.

What the sensors measure

Each sensor reports a known quantity rotated into the body frame, corrupted by a bias $b$ and noise $n$:

$$\omega_m = \underbrace{\omega_{\text{true}}}_{\text{gyro: rotation rate}} + b_g + n_g$$ $$\mathbf{a}_m = \underbrace{R(q)\,\mathbf{g}}_{\text{accel: gravity } 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2} + \mathbf{a}_{\text{linear}} + n_a$$ $$\mathbf{m}_m = \underbrace{R(q)\,\mathbf{m}_\oplus}_{\text{mag: Earth's field }\sim 50\,\mu\text{T}} + b_m + n_m$$

$R(q)$ is the rotation matrix of $q$. Noise here is colored (AR(1)) plus a drifting bias — real IMUs, not clean white noise. The gyro's bias is why integrating it alone drifts forever.

Gyro integration (the motion model)

The quaternion evolves as

$$\dot{q} = \tfrac12\, q \otimes [0,\; \omega]$$

integrated each step and renormalized. Fast and smooth, but any constant bias in $\omega$ integrates into an unbounded angle error — that's the gyro-only cube drifting.

Accelerometer tilt

At rest the accelerometer measures only gravity, so tilt follows by geometry:

$$\text{roll} = \operatorname{atan2}(a_y,\, a_z)\qquad \text{pitch} = \operatorname{atan2}(-a_x,\, \sqrt{a_y^2+a_z^2})$$

No integration, no drift — but yaw is unobservable (spinning about gravity changes nothing the accelerometer sees), and linear acceleration contaminates the reading. The accel-only cube jitters under motion and never turns.

Magnetometer heading

The compass measures the Earth's field in the body frame. Tilt it back to horizontal using the accel roll/pitch, and the yaw follows:

$$\psi = \operatorname{atan2}(-m'_y,\, m'_x)\quad\text{with}\quad m' = \text{tilt-corrected } \mathbf{m}_m$$

Bounded and drift-free, but noisy and perturbed by local metal. This is what finally gives us yaw — the whole point of adding the sensor.

Complementary filter

Blend the smooth-but-drifting gyro attitude with the jittery-but- bounded accel/mag tilt. One line, one knob:

$$q = \operatorname{slerp}\big(q_{\text{tilt}}^{(\psi)},\; q_{\text{gyro}},\; \alpha\big)$$

$\alpha$ is the trust knob: . The sliders write it straight into the editable code's params block.

The EKF: full 6-DOF pose

Everything above estimates orientation. A pose is 6-DOF — three translations and three rotations — so the filter's state is position, velocity and attitude together:

$$x = \big(\underbrace{\mathbf{p}}_{\text{world position}},\; \underbrace{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{world velocity}},\; \underbrace{q}_{\text{attitude}}\big)$$

The IMU is not treated as a sensor here — it's the filter's input. The gyro drives attitude and the accelerometer, once rotated into the world frame and stripped of gravity, drives velocity and position. That's a strapdown propagation, and it is the part that drifts:

$$\mathbf{a} = R(q)\,\mathbf{a}_m - \mathbf{g}\qquad \mathbf{p}_k = \mathbf{p}_{k-1} + \mathbf{v}_{k-1}dt + \tfrac12 \mathbf{a}\,dt^2\qquad \mathbf{v}_k = \mathbf{v}_{k-1} + \mathbf{a}\,dt\qquad q_k = q_{k-1} \otimes [1,\; \omega\tfrac{dt}{2}]$$
Why the covariance is on the error, not the state

The state has 10 numbers ($3+3+4$) but only 9 degrees of freedom — a quaternion is constrained to the unit sphere, so a Gaussian can't live on it. The standard fix is the error-state (indirect) EKF: keep the big nonlinear state as a plain estimate, and track the covariance of a small 9-vector error about it:

$$\delta x = \big(\delta\mathbf{p},\; \delta\mathbf{v},\; \delta\theta\big) \in \mathbb{R}^9 \qquad P = \operatorname{cov}(\delta x)\ \ (9\times 9)$$

$\delta\theta$ is a small body-frame rotation, related to the true attitude by $q_{\text{true}} = q \otimes [1,\, \delta\theta/2]$. It lives in a flat space, so all the usual Kalman algebra applies, and corrections are composed back onto $q$ rather than added into its $[x,y,z,w]$ numbers — an additive correction leaks into axes it shouldn't touch (that's what the heading-leakage regression check pins down).

Predict: error dynamics and $F$

Linearizing the propagation above about the current estimate gives the 9×9 transition matrix, in 3×3 blocks:

$$F = \begin{bmatrix} I & I\,dt & 0\\ 0 & I & -R(q)[\mathbf{a}_m]_\times dt\\ 0 & 0 & I - [\omega]_\times dt \end{bmatrix} \qquad Q = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & \sigma_a^2\,dt\,I & 0\\ 0 & 0 & \sigma_g^2\,dt\,I\end{bmatrix} \qquad P_k = F P_{k-1} F^{\top} + Q$$

Read it block by block. Position error grows with velocity error ($I\,dt$). Attitude error just rotates with the body ($I - [\omega]_\times dt$). The interesting one is the middle-right block: tilting the estimate by $\delta\theta$ mis-rotates the measured specific force by $\mathbf{a}_m \times \delta\theta$, leaking gravity into the horizontal axes — which is exactly why a position measurement can end up correcting attitude. That coupling is what makes the 6-DOF filter more than three separate ones. .

Update: three measurements, one recipe

Each measurement compares what the sensor should read, $h(x)$, against what it did read, $z$. The algebra is identical every time; only $h$ and its Jacobian $H = \partial h / \partial \delta x$ change:

$$y = z - h(x)\qquad S = H P H^{\top} + R\qquad K = P H^{\top} S^{-1} \qquad \delta x = K y \qquad P \leftarrow (I - KH)\,P$$

and the corrections go back in as $\mathbf{p} \mathrel{+}= \delta\mathbf{p}$, $\mathbf{v} \mathrel{+}= \delta\mathbf{v}$, $q \mathrel{\otimes}= [1,\, \delta\theta/2]$. The three sensors differ only in which columns of $H$ they touch:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{gravity (when } \lVert \mathbf{a}_m\rVert \approx g):\quad & h = R(q)^{\top}\mathbf{g}, & H &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & [h]_\times\end{bmatrix}\\ \text{compass:}\quad & h = R(q)^{\top}\mathbf{m}_\oplus, & H &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & [h]_\times\end{bmatrix}\\ \text{position fix:}\quad & h = \mathbf{p}, & H &= \begin{bmatrix} I & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix} \end{aligned}$$

For the two vector measurements, rotating the body by $\delta\theta$ moves the predicted reading by $h \times \delta\theta$, so $H = [h]_\times$ falls straight out. The gravity update is gated: the accelerometer is already the input to the velocity integration, and it only says something about tilt while the phone isn't accelerating — under motion, linear acceleration masquerades as tilt, so the update is skipped unless $\big|\lVert \mathbf{a}_m \rVert - g\big|$ is small. .

Observability: why the position fix matters

Attitude is self-correcting from gravity and the compass, both of which are bounded and always available. Position and velocity are not: nothing in an IMU measures them, so they come purely from that double integration, and a constant accelerometer bias $b$ becomes a position error of

$$\Delta \mathbf{p}(t) = \tfrac12\, b\, t^2$$

— quadratic, unbounded, hopeless within seconds. The demo therefore feeds the filter a slow (2 Hz), noisy position fix, standing in for GPS, UWB, vision or wheel odometry. That single measurement is what makes the translation states observable, and through the $-R(q)[\mathbf{a}_m]_\times$ coupling it sharpens roll and pitch too. What Q and R do

Two knobs, and everything the filter does sits between them:

  • High $R$ (little trust in a sensor): the filter barely corrects, so $K \to 0$ and the estimate stays smooth but can drift. Raise $R_{\text{mag}}$ and watch yaw wander again; raise $R_{\text{pos}}$ and position walks off like dead reckoning.
  • High $Q$ (little trust in the IMU): $P$ grows, so $K$ grows with it and the filter snaps to the measurements — jittery, but never drifts. The covariance plot shows both blocks of $P$: the attitude trace shrinking on each accel/mag update, the position trace sawtoothing as it regrows between fixes and collapses at each one.
